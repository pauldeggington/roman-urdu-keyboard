Most Roman Urdu typing is a compromise: you either write in English, or you spell Urdu out letter by letter and hope it reads right.

Roman Urdu Keyboard does the translating for you, in the tone you choose, while everything stays on your phone.

Say it in the right tone

Casual, formal, poetic, funny or respectful, picked from a word on the toolbar. Or leave it on Auto and the keyboard reads the situation. Male or female wording too: thak gaya or thak gayi.

badha→barha بڑھا
parna→parhna پڑھنا
pool→phool پھول

Spelling that follows the Urdu letters

There is no standard Roman Urdu spelling, so the keyboard follows the Urdu script instead of Hindi habits. Add your own in the glossary (bada → barha) and it uses them every time.

Emoji, GIFs, stickers, clipboard

An emoji screen with search and skin tones, text emoticons in groups, GIFs and stickers from GIPHY, and a clipboard history like Gboard's. Tap to paste, long-press to pin.

Types like Gboard

Same layout and height, glide typing in English, word suggestions, autocorrect you can undo with one Backspace, and next-word hints. Light, dark, or your wallpaper colours.

Voice, both ways

Share an Urdu voice note from WhatsApp to the app and read it in English. Or speak English and get an Urdu voice message to send. The listening runs on the phone, with the Gemma 4 model.

Works offline

The translator is a Gemma model that downloads once (0.6 GB or 2.6 GB, your choice) and then runs with no connection at all.

Private by design

What you type never leaves the phone. No account, no analytics, no ads. Learned words stay on the device, and never in password fields.

Open source

The whole keyboard is on GitHub. Read the code, check the signing fingerprint, or build it yourself.

No. Translation, suggestions, autocorrect and learning all run on the phone. The only things that use the internet are the one-time model download, GIF and sticker search (your search words go to GIPHY), and the keyboard's mic button, which uses Android's own speech recognition. Everything is in the privacy policy.

The app itself is small. The translator downloads once during setup: Gemma 4 E2B is about 2.6 GB and gives the best translations, Gemma 3 1B is about 0.6 GB, faster but less accurate. Use Wi-Fi. After that, nothing else downloads.

Android 12 or newer on a 64-bit phone, with a few gigabytes free for the model. It was built and tested on a Pixel 8a. Newer phones with more memory translate faster.

The model has to load into memory after a restart or after the keyboard has been idle, then it stays warm while you type. If it still feels slow, turn on Use the GPU in the app or switch to the smaller model.

There is no standard. The keyboard follows the Urdu letters, so words with ڑھ become rh and words with پھ become ph. Add your own spellings in the app's glossary and they are used every time.
